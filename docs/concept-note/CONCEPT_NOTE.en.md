## 1. Problem and why it matters

A land transaction in Bangladesh passes through offices that do not share a live view of the record. The sub-registry registers the deed, the AC Land office decides mutation, and the survey records (CS, SA, RS and BS khatians) sit in other systems. Nobody cross-checks them at the moment a sale or a mutation is accepted. The khatian alone is not conclusive proof of ownership [S1], and reform literature calls for inter-checking between the Sub-Registrar and AC Land offices [S2].

The gaps this leaves are concrete: the same share can be sold twice, a seller can sell more than they own, a deed can be signed for land the record shows as khas, and a mutation can change a different area than the deed transfers. Land disputes are a major source of civil litigation [S3, check the figure before quoting it]. Existing e-services improve access, but many digitize the process without screening for conflicts [S4]. In our own literature search we found many blockchain land-registry proposals and little on Bangladesh-specific, pre-transaction, explainable fraud screening [S5; this is our search, not a systematic review].

**Who uses it:** the Sub-Registrar (primary), the AC Land officer (mutation), and citizens and buyers (a plain-language check before they pay). Banks and courts are later users.

## 2. Our solution

**Ardh Guard** is an explainable conflict-detection layer that works at two checkpoints: **before registration** and **before mutation**. It compares a deed with the record chain, the seller's earlier deeds and the mutation history, and flags the case for a human officer **with plain-language reasons**. It never decides ownership.

It runs eight checks: land sold more than once; selling more than the seller owns; government (khas) land; seller not on the record; altered document (QR or signature); mutation that does not match the deed; deed never mutated; mutation resubmitted after rejection. Each check is a readable rule, so an officer can explain a refusal to a citizen.

## 3. How it works

_Figure 1: use `assets/flow.svg` exported as PNG._ A deed or mutation request arrives, Ardh Guard compares it with the record, an alert with reasons is raised, and an officer confirms the hold, dismisses it, or escalates.

- **Engine:** pure TypeScript functions. Input: registry (deeds, khatian chain, mutations). Output: alerts with structured reasons, which the interface phrases in Bangla or English.
- **Interface:** React, Vite and Tailwind CSS, in Bangla by default with an English toggle; hosted on Vercel. Views for citizen / buyer, officer / admin, or both.
- **Data:** a fictional registry generated by a script (3 mouzas, 24 parcels, 96 khatians, 25 deeds, 23 mutation cases, 9 planted anomalies). No real people, NID numbers, phone numbers or land records; nothing scraped.
- **Evaluation:** on the planted anomalies the rules caught 8 of 8 testable cases and raised no alerts outside them. We present this as a **regression test, not accuracy**: the data is small and the rules were written knowing it.

## 4. Main features

- Check a deed before registration, with a hold / review / clear verdict and reasons.
- Alert inbox for officers with confirm hold, dismiss and escalate.
- Parcel pages showing the CS, SA, RS, BS chain, deeds and mutations together.
- Citizen "check before you buy" page that shows no details of other people's deeds.
- Bangla (default) and English; citizen, officer and both views.
- In-app benchmark, how-it-works, roadmap and limits pages.

## 5. Using it across the country

1. **Pilot:** one sub-registry office and its AC Land office, under a formal data-sharing agreement.
2. **Scale:** upazila by upazila, then district. The engine runs in the browser and needs little infrastructure, and the interface is Bangla-first.
3. **Prerequisite:** clean, linked parcel history. This is the hardest open question (resolving plots across surveys), and we say so openly.
4. **Governance:** officers keep every decision; sign-in, roles and an audit trail come before any real use.

## 6. What we will do next (plans, none built yet)

- **v1.1 (final round):** coherent-chain test data and a chain-break rule; owner alerts and dispute hold; saved decisions with an audit trail; sign-in and roles; Bangla name matching; a "messy but innocent" benchmark to measure false alarms; **AI: plain-language case summaries in Bangla and English** using a pretrained language model, always reviewed by the officer.
- **v2:** plot resolution across surveys; pilot; **AI: Bangla OCR** (pretrained) to read scanned khatians and deeds with officer confirmation; **AI: machine-learning risk ranking after the rules** once realistic data exists, keeping the rules and reasons; mortgage, court-case and land-tax checks for citizens.

**Limitations:** dummy data only; small benchmark; chain-break detection untested; exact name matching; the role switch is a demo control, not access control. **No AI model runs in the prototype today.**
